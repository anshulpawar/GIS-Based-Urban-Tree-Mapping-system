SCIENTIFIC & TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION

Remote Sensing & Analytical Methodology

A rigorous technical reference detailing Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance ingestion, cloud masking algorithms, deterministic NDVI classification, and canopy health index formulation.

1. Satellite Telemetry, Spectral Bands & Cloud Masking

Urban GreenWatch queries the European Space Agency (ESA) Copernicus Sentinel-2 twin-satellite constellation (Sentinel-2A and Sentinel-2B), providing an orbital revisit period of 5 days at the equator and 2–3 days at mid-latitudes. The system ingests calibrated Level-2A Bottom-Of-Atmosphere (BOA) surface reflectance, correcting for atmospheric aerosol scattering and water vapor absorption.

Sentinel-2 Band Central Wavelength Spatial Resolution Primary Remote Sensing Function
Band 4 (Red) 665 nm 10 meters Strong chlorophyll absorption by healthy green vegetation foliage.
Band 8 (NIR) 842 nm 10 meters High cellular reflectance by spongy mesophyll leaf structure.
QA60 Bitmask Multi-band Flag 60 meters Opaque cloud and cirrus detection flag for pixel-level exclusion.
SCL (Scene Class) Multi-band Discrete 20 meters Identifies cloud shadows, defective pixels, water bodies, and snow.

Pixel-Level Cloud Filtering: Imagery undergoes automated cloud masking via the Sentinel-2 QA60 bitmask (bit 10 for opaque clouds, bit 11 for cirrus) and Scene Classification Layer (SCL). Pixels flagged as high cloud probability or cloud shadows are omitted during spatial aggregation to eliminate radiometric distortion.

2. Deterministic NDVI & 4-Tier Canopy Classification

The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) mathematically contrasts chlorophyll absorption in the visible Red spectrum with strong reflectance in the Near-Infrared spectrum:

NDVI = (Band 8 NIR - Band 4 Red) / (Band 8 NIR + Band 4 Red) Where Band 8 (NIR) = 842 nm, Band 4 (Red) = 665 nm, yielding values strictly bounded between -1.0 and +1.0.

To translate continuous radiometric values into actionable municipal planning categories, pixels within the AOI boundary are segmented into 4 standardized density bins:

Canopy Classification Tier NDVI Threshold Ground Characteristic Urban Ecological Function
Dense Tree Canopy ≥ 0.60 Contiguous mature tree crowns, urban forests, dense parks Primary urban cooling, maximum stormwater interception, carbon sequestration
Moderate Canopy / Shrubs 0.40 – 0.59 Light tree canopy, hedgerows, domestic gardens, young plantings Secondary shade, air particulate filtration, localized microclimate buffering
Sparse / Open Ground 0.20 – 0.39 Grass verges, athletic turf, open vacant ground, agricultural fields Soil permeability, low thermal mitigation, prime infill planting opportunities
Built-up / Deficit < 0.20 Impervious roads, rooftops, paved concrete, bare compacted soil Heat island amplification, zero canopy benefit, priority deficit intervention zones

Green Cover Benchmark: Total vegetative cover is defined as the combined surface area of Dense and Moderate tiers (NDVI ≥ 0.30 or ≥ 0.40 depending on municipal criteria). Areas falling below the WHO 30% urban canopy benchmark are classified as Green Deficit zones, triggering planting calculations of ~400 native shade trees per hectare of deficit.

3. Deterministic Canopy Health Score & Temporal Change Math

To provide a unified, transparent metric of urban forest vitality, Urban GreenWatch computes a deterministic Canopy Health Score (0–100) balancing photosynthetic vigor with spatial footprint extent:

Health Score = clamp(5, 100, round((Mean NDVI × 60) + (Green Cover % × 0.4))) Vigor Weight = 60% (mean photosynthetic vitality across AOI) • Spatial Extent Weight = 40% (percentage of AOI with NDVI ≥ 0.30).

Score Interpretation Bands:

Score Range Classification Ecological Condition
< 45 / 100 Critical Deficit High impervious surface, extreme heat vulnerability, urgent afforestation required.
45 – 64 / 100 Moderate Vigor Patchy canopy, lawn-dominated or young saplings; requires canopy density infill.
65 – 79 / 100 Good Canopy Substantial mature tree cover meeting baseline urban resilience criteria.
80 – 100 / 100 Optimal Canopy Contiguous, multi-tiered mature urban forest providing maximum ecosystem services.

Multi-Date Temporal Comparison: When analyzing two Sentinel-2 acquisitions (T1 baseline and T2 current), change is computed on identical AOI geometry:

ΔNDVI = Mean NDVI(T2) - Mean NDVI(T1) ΔGreen Cover % = Green Cover %(T2) - Green Cover %(T1) Positive values represent vegetation gain or seasonal greening; negative values indicate tree clearance or leaf senescence.

4. Remote Sensing Scope & Honest Scientific Limitations

Transparency and scientific integrity are paramount. Users should evaluate Urban GreenWatch telemetry within the following verified bounds of satellite remote sensing:

10m Mixed-Pixel Footprints (No Individual Tree Stems): Each Sentinel-2 pixel integrates electromagnetic radiance over a 10m × 10m (100 m²) ground area. The system measures aggregate vegetative canopy cover and vigor; it does not count individual tree stems, measure trunk diameters (DBH), or resolve isolated street saplings smaller than the pixel dimension.
No Optical Species Identification from Satellite: 10-meter broadband optical sensors cannot classify tree species. All recommended flora provided by Urban GreenWatch are derived from deterministic horticultural rules based on geographic location, elevation, climate zone, and empirical NDVI density—not optical spectral classification.
Phenology & Seasonal Variation: NDVI values naturally fluctuate across seasons due to precipitation cycles, deciduous leaf shed, and agricultural harvesting. For rigorous multi-date change detection, always compare imagery acquired during similar seasons or phenological windows.
Understory and Lawn Contribution: Well-watered golf courses, agricultural plots, or dense ground lawns can exhibit high NDVI values. The Canopy Health Score incorporates both mean NDVI and green cover thresholds to mitigate lawn bias, but field ground-truthing remains essential for municipal planning.

Methodology & Satellite Remote Sensing

Sentinel-2 Surface Reflectance (L2A)

The system processes high-resolution multispectral observations captured by the European Space Agency's Copernicus Sentinel-2 constellation at 10-meter spatial resolution. Cloud and cirrus pixels are filtered using the QA60 cloud-masking bitmask to guarantee surface radiance accuracy.

Deterministic NDVI Formula

The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) quantifies vegetation canopy density by comparing Near-Infrared (NIR) reflectance to Red visible light absorption:

NDVI = (Band 8 NIR - Band 4 Red) / (Band 8 NIR + Band 4 Red)

Where Band 8 (NIR) corresponds to 842 nm and Band 4 (Red) corresponds to 665 nm.

Canopy Cover vs. Individual Stems

Optical satellite sensing evaluates aggregate tree canopy coverage and vegetative density across each 10m × 10m ground pixel. The system maps broad leaf foliage, green cover percentage, and canopy health rather than identifying individual tree stems or trunks.

Urban Forestry Benchmarks

Analyzed boundaries are algorithmically compared against the WHO 30% urban canopy guideline, identifying municipal green deficits and calculating planting requirements (~400 shade trees per hectare of deficit).
